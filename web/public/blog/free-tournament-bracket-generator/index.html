Free Online Tournament Bracket Generator — No Account Required

Running a tournament bracket shouldn't require signing up for yet another service, learning another tool, or paying a monthly subscription. PachinkoMaster's tournament mode handles the full bracket — seeding, qualifying rounds, head-to-head matchups, automatic progression — and it's completely free with no account needed.

Two Tournament Formats, One Tool

PachinkoMaster offers two distinct tournament modes, each suited to different situations.

Elimination Mode

In Elimination mode, all players compete in every round. After each drop, the players who scored lowest are cut. You control how many are eliminated per round — from one player at a time (slow burn, maximum drama) up to half the field (rapid-fire competition). The last player standing wins one title; the player with the highest cumulative points across all rounds wins another. Both titles can go to the same player or different ones, which creates interesting strategic tension as the tournament progresses.

Elimination mode is ideal when you want everyone to play for multiple rounds before the field narrows, and when cumulative performance should matter alongside survival.

Knockout Bracket Mode

Knockout Bracket mode is the classic single-elimination format. Players are seeded and matched head-to-head. The winner of each match advances; the loser is out. The bracket view shows the full tournament tree — who's playing who, who has advanced, and who's waiting. It auto-progresses as each match completes.

For the bracket to work cleanly, you need a power-of-2 number of players (8, 16, 32, 64). If your group isn't that size, PachinkoMaster handles the gap with a qualifying round: odd players out compete for the remaining spots before the main bracket begins.

What Makes a Fair Bracket

Seeding matters enormously in any single-elimination bracket. If you seed randomly, you risk putting your two strongest competitors against each other in the first round — which wastes what should be a final match and demoralises everyone else. A properly seeded bracket ensures the strongest players are on opposite sides of the draw and only meet in the later rounds.

PachinkoMaster's qualifying round addresses the "odd number out" problem cleanly. Rather than giving some players a bye (which feels unfair and often isn't transparent), it runs a preliminary round where everyone competes and the field narrows to the nearest power of 2. Only after that qualifying round does the seeded bracket begin.

Seeding tip: You can customise seed order manually before the bracket begins. If you're running a competitive tournament where prior rankings exist (seasonal standings, previous tournament results), enter players in seed order so the bracket reflects real competitive balance.

Setting Up Your Bracket in Under 5 Minutes

  1. Open PachinkoMaster and go to the Players panel. Add all participants — type them in or paste a list.
  2. Set your prizes. For a bracket tournament, you want clear point differentials: a high-value "Win" slot and a low-value "Loss" slot is the simplest setup. You can also add "Bonus" slots for extra drama.
  3. Enable Tournament Mode and select Knockout Bracket from the mode options.
  4. Review the seeding. PachinkoMaster shows you the bracket draw before you start. Adjust seed positions if needed.
  5. Run the qualifying round if your player count isn't a power of 2. This resolves automatically.
  6. Run the bracket. Each match is a ball drop — press "Drop All" and the physics decides the winner. Advance to the next match with one click.

Good Use Cases

Office sports pools

For World Cup sweepstakes, office football pools, or March Madness brackets, PachinkoMaster's knockout mode generates the bracket and lets you run rounds as the real-world tournament progresses. Each round of the physics bracket maps to a round of the actual competition.

Classroom competitions

Spelling bees, maths challenges, quiz knockouts — any academic competition where students compete in rounds benefits from a bracket structure. The visual format makes the competition stakes clear and motivating.

Game nights

Running a board game tournament, a card game league, or a video game competition? Use PachinkoMaster to generate the bracket and track results across sessions. The shared URL means everyone can see the bracket from their own devices.

Exporting and Sharing Results

At the end of the tournament, hit Export CSV to download the complete results — every player, every round score, final standings. The bracket URL also stays shareable throughout the tournament, so you can post it in a group chat and everyone can follow along even if they're not in the room.
